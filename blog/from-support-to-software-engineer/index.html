From Software Support to Software Engineer: How My Thinking Changed

Years of talking to real users taught me things no course did. Here is what I carried from the support desk into engineering.

Support teaches you what users actually do

On a support desk, nobody describes the system the way the documentation does. People tell you what they were trying to achieve and what happened instead. That gap between intention and behaviour is where most real bugs, and most good product ideas, are hiding.

Debugging is a communication skill

More often than not the problem is not the code, it is understanding the problem. A few habits that followed me from support into engineering:

  • Reproduce the issue before you start theorising.
  • Ask what changed, because something always did.
  • Write the fix down so the next person does not start from zero.
A bug report is a story. Your job is to find the missing chapter.

What I carry into engineering

  • Empathy for the person on the other side of the screen.
  • A preference for simple, observable systems.
  • The habit of documenting every solution.

What comes next

Today I am investing seriously in Python and AI, with the same mindset: start from a real problem, then pick the tool.
